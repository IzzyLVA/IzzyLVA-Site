06

DIGITAL PRIVACY / PUBLIC INTEREST

SMILE FOR THE CAMERA.
PRIVACY IS NOT A CRIME.

THE QUESTION ISN’T JUST WHAT TECHNOLOGY CAN DO. IT’S WHO IT SERVES, WHAT IT COLLECTS, AND WHO ANSWERS FOR IT.

Surveillance is no longer limited to cameras on a street corner.

It can live in the apps we use, the devices we carry, the locations we share, and the data collected about our everyday lives. Convenience should not mean giving up every expectation of privacy.

01 / Privacy matters

Privacy is not about having something to hide. It is about having dignity, boundaries, and a say in how information about you is collected and used.

02 / Question collection

Data can improve services and help protect people. But collection should have a clear purpose, reasonable limits, and an explanation people can actually understand.

03 / Demand accountability

Governments and companies can hold significant amounts of personal information. That power should come with transparency, oversight, and meaningful consequences for misuse.

04 / Security protects people

Strong security helps prevent personal information from being exposed, abused, or taken out of context. Protecting systems also means considering the people who depend on them.

05 / Consent should mean something

People deserve clear choices about their information—not confusing settings, hidden defaults, or the false impression that using an essential service means agreeing to everything.

06 / Balance power with care

There are legitimate reasons to monitor systems and investigate threats. The goal is not to reject technology, but to ask whether its use is necessary, proportionate, and accountable.

Understand the systems that observe us.

Build and support systems that protect people.

Question what collects your data. Understand what protects it.

A THOUGHT TO LEAVE YOU WITH

HAVE YOU EVER GOOGLED YOURSELF?

You might be surprised by what you find. The amount of data companies have on you is bigger than you think.
